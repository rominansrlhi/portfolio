Selected case study · Digikala Mehr

Why onboarding a charity partner took thirty days

Self-initiated UX research project  ·  Team: Digikala's Social Responsibility team  ·  Focus: partner onboarding

The problem

Charity partners onboarding onto Digikala Mehr often stalled or dropped off before completing the process. My initial assumption was that the questionnaire itself was simply "too long." This project set out to test that assumption — and find out what was actually making onboarding hard.

Research questions

01
At which stage of onboarding do charities get stuck most often?

Locating the point of drop-off, rather than assuming it.

02
What technical, informational, or motivational barriers are involved?

Separating UX problems from process or policy problems.

03
What expectations do charities have that the platform isn't meeting?

Surfacing the gap between what's expected and what's delivered.

Methodology

Qualitative Research

Direct conversations with 20+ charity partners to understand why they struggled to complete the onboarding questionnaire and identify recurring pain points.

Process & Timeline Analysis

Used ClickUp to track the duration of each onboarding stage and identify where charities experienced delays and drop-off.

Retrospective Analysis

Compared onboarding performance before and after changes to the questionnaire and documentation requirements.

Examples of questions used in follow-up conversations

  • What made it difficult to complete the questionnaire?
  • Were there any questions you couldn't answer immediately? Why?
  • Did you need to collect information or documents from other people?
  • Was there anything that made you postpone or stop completing the questionnaire?
  • What would have made the onboarding process easier for you?

Findings

01

High information dependency created friction

Finding

More than 20 charity partners reported difficulty completing the questionnaire because some required information wasn't readily available to whoever was filling it in. They often had to coordinate with managers or other internal teams to get it.

Insight

The onboarding flow implicitly assumed one person could access everything it asked for. In reality, onboarding was a multi-stakeholder task — progress depended on internal coordination the platform never accounted for.

UX implication

The flow should minimize information dependencies and make clear, up front, exactly what will be needed before someone starts.

02

Questionnaire length increased completion effort

Finding

The questionnaire held around 50 questions, and charities consistently described it as long and time-consuming. Making 20 of those questions optional cut average completion time from an average of 30 days to an average of 20 days.

Insight

Not every question carried equal weight for onboarding. Treating everything as mandatory inflated the perceived effort and gave people a reason to postpone finishing.

UX implication

Prioritize what's essential, and make the line between required and optional visible, not just technically true.

03

Documentation requirements added unnecessary burden

Finding

Charities struggled to prepare the documents the flow requested. After reviewing the list with the Legal team, several non-essential documents were removed.

Insight

Onboarding was asking for effort — information and paperwork — that wasn't always necessary to move the process forward.

UX implication

Every requested document should have a clear purpose, and should only be asked for at the stage it's actually needed.

04

No Save & Resume amplified the cost of interruption

Finding

When charities had to step away to gather missing information or documents, they couldn't save their progress. Their answers were lost, and they had to restart the entire questionnaire.

Insight

The real problem wasn't only how much was being asked — it was the cost of stepping away. A task that already required coordination across people became a high-risk, all-or-nothing activity.

UX implication

A Save & Resume mechanism could lower the cost of interruption and reduce drop-off tied to it.

Unresolved opportunity — this was identified during the research but not addressed within the project's scope. It's presented here as a recommendation for future work, not something already implemented.
The strongest overall insight

Onboarding was designed as a single-session task, but charities experienced it as a multi-stakeholder process requiring time, coordination, and access to information and documents.
